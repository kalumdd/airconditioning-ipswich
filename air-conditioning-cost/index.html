What changes the price

  • System size and type: a 2.5 kW bedroom unit costs less than a 5 kW living-room unit; multi-splits and ducted systems cost more.
  • Brand and efficiency: premium ranges are quieter and more efficient but cost more up front.
  • Pipe run: most quotes include 3–5 m; longer runs, loft routes and high walls add time and materials.
  • Outdoor unit position: wall brackets at height, roof mounting or awkward access add cost.
  • Electrical work: a straightforward new circuit is included; a consumer unit upgrade is extra.
  • Condensate: gravity drainage is simple; a pump adds a little.
  • Making good: decorating, boxing in pipework or coring thick walls in older buildings.

What our quotes include

  • The make, model and kW of each unit, with efficiency ratings
  • All pipework, trunking, brackets or stands and the condensate route
  • The electrical supply, pressure test, vacuum and commissioning
  • Warranty, lead time, payment terms and how long the quote is valid
  • The price per extra metre of pipe, so nothing comes as a surprise

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Choose a wall-mounted split where it suits the room: it is the best-value system.
  • Put the indoor and outdoor units back to back on the same wall.
  • Do several rooms at once with a multi-split, rather than one at a time.
  • Get a quote by video: it saves a visit and is quicker.

Running costs

At the Ofgem price cap from 1 October 2026 (26.32p per kWh for electricity), a typical 3.5 kW system cooling a room costs around 7–8p an hour once up to temperature. Heating with the same system costs roughly a quarter of electric heaters. Try the running cost calculator.
